# Style guide

## 1. How GeoGlobeTales writes

Learned from 5 of his scripts (Canada's lakes, Broadway, the 13 colonies, the Louisiana
Purchase, beef vs chicken) and his channel https://www.youtube.com/@GeoGlobeTales (~2M
followers). **Copy the structure, never his phrases.**

### The core loop
Weird thing you can see → "how?" → go back in time (a year) → a chain of events with people's
motives and funny inner thoughts → payoff connected to today.

He often gives away **what** happened, but never **how**. The curiosity is "how on earth?".

### Openings (he changes the move every video)

| Type | Shape |
|---|---|
| Fact plus a problem | "[Place], home to [huge number of X], and at some point that became a real problem for [people]." |
| "Look at this", then the oddity, then a question | Show a map, point at the thing that breaks the pattern, then "So how did this happen?" |
| Direct "why" question | "Why did [unlikely thing] happen?" |
| Spoil the ending, then rewind | "[Small player] somehow ended up with [huge result]. But let's start from the beginning." |
| "Have you ever wondered…" plus the odd one out | A list where one item doesn't fit |

### Techniques
1. **Present tense, like it's happening now.** A year on its own starts a scene, like a movie cut: "1802. France and Britain make peace…"
2. **Casual modern thoughts for historical people.** This is his main humor tool, e.g. the Dutch looking at a trail: "Cool, they built us a road."
3. **Dry sarcasm and understatement, never loud jokes.** Dark facts dropped casually hit harder.
4. **Conversational glue words:** "You see," "Well," "So," "But then," "Of course," "At the same time". It sounds like a friend explaining, not a documentary.
5. **He asks the question the viewer would ask, then answers it with a joke.**
6. **Simple cause-and-effect chains:** this happened → so they did this → but then → so → and as a result. No descriptive filler sentences at all.
7. **Escalation:** each step tops the last one (his Canada lake names get sillier and sillier).
8. **The ending is a punchline or a "wow" number, never a moral.** No "follow for more", no "and that's why it matters".
9. **Plain words.** No "stunning", "quietly", "secret weapon" or dramatic metaphors. The drama comes from the facts, the fun from the tone.

The middle is either a **timeline** (Louisiana, Broadway) or an **escalating list** (Canada lakes).

## 2. Rules for our scripts

Each of these came from feedback on a draft:

- **Copy structure, never phrases.** Using his signature lines ("Look,", "literally", "a disaster caused by humans themselves", "And as a result, it worked") makes us look like a copy.
- **Plain, spoken words.** "Writer" phrases sound AI-made. Short sentences, like telling a friend.
- **But not flat.** Keep tension, vivid moments and a bit of attitude ("Big mistake." "Crops died. Cattle died.").
- **Open loops, not answers.** Don't give the answer straight away; hold the reveal to the end. Lines like "Until people made one small decision…" keep people watching.
- **Very few questions to the viewer.** One in the hook at most.
- **Dates as scene anchors:** "1970s." "1983."
- **Every number is checked against a source before rendering.** The Dust Bowl script we were sent had 3 wrong facts (dust walls "2.5 m" high when they rose over 3 km; a shelterbelt "100 m" wide when it was ~100 miles; a disputed death toll). Accuracy is a selling point.
- **Spell out decades for the TTS:** "nineteen seventies", not "1970s" (Kokoro reads that as "nineteen hundred seventies").
- **50–75 seconds.**

### Story formula (the Dust Bowl / shelterbelt script)
**Paradox → place → change → hidden cause → escalating disaster → clever solution → payoff.**
Every sentence raises a question or answers the last one.

## 3. What the top map Shorts do on screen

From Gemini's breakdowns of 7 videos with 1.5M–15M views (Just Planet, Sach Side, MapLore; full
notes in `reference-analyses/`):

1. **Hook in the first 3 seconds:** a surprising claim plus a fast zoom ("Have you ever noticed…", a superlative, or a myth-buster).
2. **Something changes every 1.5–3 seconds**, and the camera never stops moving.
3. **Dark satellite or 3D terrain base** with bright, color-coded countries (red, blue, yellow) and glowing borders.
4. **Word-by-word captions**, bold white, key words in yellow, lower center.
5. **A sound effect on every action:** whoosh on camera moves, pop on labels, thud on big numbers, themed sounds (glass shattering when an empire falls).
6. **Quiet music:** lo-fi or phonk (Just Planet), dark synth (Sach Side), orchestral war music (MapLore).
7. **A deep, energetic male narrator**, probably ElevenLabs.
8. **Scale comparisons:** Chile laid over Europe, France inside Mongolia.
9. **Map graphics:** white-box labels, pins, flags, dotted route arrows.
10. **Structure:** hook, escalation ("but that's just the beginning…"), punchy last line.

The 15M-view Just Planet video ("A House in Two Countries") uses **no cartoons at all**, just
satellite zooms, colored countries, labels, a whoosh per zoom, a pop per label and a lo-fi beat.

The Chile reference (MapLore-style) adds humor that ours lacked at first: a joke in the first 2
seconds, cartoon flags with eyes that talk, several voices, kinetic text ("LOOOOONG"), and a
comedy sting on every joke.
